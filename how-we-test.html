How ToolNest tests and researches tools

What the evidence labels mean, how decisions are written, and when a page is updated.

Two different evidence labels

ToolNest uses plain labels because reading a vendor page is not the same as using a product. Hands-on tested and Researched describe different kinds of evidence. A reader should be able to tell which one supports a sentence before treating it as a product experience.

What Hands-on tested means

A page may use the Hands-on tested label only when Abdul personally uses the product through a real account or a real product workflow. A sign-up screen, a demo video or a vendor claim is not a hands-on test. The page should say what was used, not imply that every feature or plan was examined.

The minimum working standard is at least three real tasks that represent the product's stated job. The tasks should be recorded in the research notes, along with the test date and the plan or account level used. Where it is useful and safe to publish them, the page may include screenshots owned by ToolNest. Screenshots are not required on every page, but the wording must never imply that screenshots, tasks or results exist if they do not.

Hands-on testing does not mean a product is good, and it does not produce a universal score. It records an observed workflow with limits. A test can reveal friction, missing controls or an unexpected restriction; it cannot prove that every user's experience will be identical. Product changes can make an old observation stale, so the test date matters.

What Researched means

Researched means the page was assembled from public material rather than a claimed personal trial. The normal sources are the vendor's product pages, documentation, help centre, pricing page, plan limits, legal terms and change notes. Commercial facts are dated because prices, quotas, free plans and billing terms change.

A researched page distinguishes a vendor-stated fact from ToolNest's editorial interpretation. “The vendor lists” is different from “ToolNest observed”. If a page cannot verify a number on an official source, it should not present that number as settled fact. Readers should confirm current terms at the official site before purchase.

How scores and verdicts work

ToolNest does not use a universal numeric score, star rating or hidden ranking formula. A verdict is a written fit decision: which job the tool can serve, what trade-off matters, who should skip it and which alternative may be better. The conclusion follows the evidence and the stated use case, not an affiliate rate or a claim that a product is best for everyone.

Comparison pages use the same approach. They identify the decision, compare relevant capabilities and limits, and state which product is the better fit for a described buyer. If the evidence is incomplete, the page should say what remains uncertain instead of filling the gap with a score.

How often pages are updated

Pages are reviewed when a material fact changes or when a correction is reported. That includes pricing, plan limits, free access, availability, product status and important terms. The updated date is a record of the latest editorial or commercial check; it is not a promise that every feature was retested on that date.

When a page is refreshed from official documentation or public pricing, the source date should be visible in the page's evidence section. A date is not added merely to make an old page look current. If a claim becomes uncertain, ToolNest can qualify it, remove it from the index, or wait for a source that supports it.
