Moz Pro vs Semrush: which SEO platform fits?

Moz Pro is the more focused choice for tracking and improving sites you own. Semrush is the broader choice when competitor, paid-search, content and AI-search research are part of the job.

The short answer

Our verdict

Choose Moz Pro when your recurring work is rank tracking, site crawling, keyword research and link research for the sites you manage. Its displayed Standard tier starts at $99 monthly, with a lower monthly equivalent on annual billing.

Choose Semrush when you will use a wider market-intelligence workflow: competitor research, paid-search research, content workflows, SEO auditing, ranking and AI-search visibility. Its displayed SEO tier starts at $139 monthly. The higher price only makes sense if those extra workflows matter to you.

Choose Moz Pro if…

  • you mainly monitor and improve your own websites
  • you want a focused SEO workspace rather than many marketing modules
  • the Standard or Medium quotas fit your tracked sites, keywords and crawl volume
  • you do not need Semrush's paid-search and broader competitor workflows

Choose Semrush if…

  • competitor, keyword, content and paid-search research are recurring tasks
  • you need several monitored websites or a larger daily keyword allowance
  • AI-search visibility and prompt tracking belong in the same workflow
  • you will use reporting, integrations or API access where your plan includes them

Moz Pro or Semrush: choose by situation

Choose Moz Pro when rank tracking, keyword research and site crawling are the recurring jobs and its quotas cover your workload. Choose Semrush when broader competitor research, SEO workflows and AI-search visibility justify the extra complexity. Choose neither when you only need one specialist SEO task.

Who should skip each tool

Skip Moz Pro if…

You need paid-search intelligence, broad competitor-market analysis, or a content-production toolkit as a core part of the subscription. Moz's official plan page presents a focused SEO and AI/GEO product, not a full paid-marketing suite.

Skip Semrush if…

You have one small site, only need occasional keyword ideas, and will not use the wider modules. Paying for unused datasets can be poor value; compare the limits you will actually reach.

Choose neither if you need only an occasional crawl or a single specialist job. A dedicated crawler or a narrower tool may fit better, but verify the current license and feature boundaries before switching.

Realistic limitations

Moz Pro

  • Lower tiers have finite site, keyword, crawl and AI quotas.
  • The official positioning is narrower than a broad paid-search and marketing suite.
  • Additional dashboards, prompts and other add-ons can increase the subscription.
  • Backlink and keyword data are vendor datasets; validate coverage in your market.

Semrush

  • Core plans have website, daily keyword, prompt and report limits.
  • Advanced API, team and reporting capabilities are higher-tier or add-on concerns.
  • The breadth of modules can increase learning time and cost.
  • The separate AI Visibility Base plan is billed per domain, not simply included in every core workflow.
