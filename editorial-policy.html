ToolNest editorial policy

The rules for independent recommendations, evidence labels, affiliate links and corrections.

Purpose and responsibility

ToolNest Editorial produces research-based pages using AI-assisted research from official vendor sources. The site exists to help readers make a more specific software decision: which tool fits a job, which trade-off is acceptable, and who should skip a product. The writing should be useful without pretending that one product is right for everyone.

ToolNest does not claim a staff of reviewers, formal credentials, awards or experience that is not stated here. Contact details are public so a reader can request a correction.

Evidence and wording

Product facts are normally checked against vendor documentation, official product pages, public pricing pages, plan limits and published terms. Pages based on those sources use Researched or source-verified wording. A vendor description remains a vendor description; it is not presented as an independent observation.

Hands-on tested is reserved for personal use through a real account and real tasks. The methodology page explains the minimum standard. ToolNest does not claim hands-on testing for a page merely because it links to a product, contains detailed copy or appears in a comparison.

Prices, limits and availability are dated snapshots. When the source is unclear, the page should say so or leave the claim out. Readers should confirm current terms on the official site.

Verdicts and who should skip

Every review or comparison should explain its verdict in the context of a job. It should also say who should skip the tool or choose a narrower alternative when that distinction is useful. A verdict is editorial fit guidance, not a promise of performance and not a universal score.

ToolNest does not accept payment for a positive verdict, higher ranking or softer criticism. A free account, partnership conversation or affiliate relationship cannot change the conclusion. If a material commercial relationship affects a page, it should be disclosed on that page.

Affiliate links

Some outbound links may earn ToolNest a commission if a reader buys or signs up. Affiliate links never change the verdict, the ranking, the recommendation, the stated limitations or the explanation of who should skip a tool. Links are marked in the page code and readers are directed to the official vendor site.

The affiliate disclosure explains the link policy in plain language. Readers can use a non-affiliate official link where one is available.

Corrections

Send corrections to toolnestext@gmail.com. Include the page URL, the exact claim, what appears wrong and an official source when possible. ToolNest Editorial checks the report, updates the wording when the evidence supports it, and may explain why a claim remains qualified.

A changed date means a page was checked or materially edited; it does not mean every feature was retested. Pages can also be marked noindex while they are too short, too uncertain or waiting for better evidence. They remain live for readers who arrive through an existing link.
