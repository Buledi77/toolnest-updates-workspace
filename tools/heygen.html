HeyGen review: avatar videos without the camera crew

HeyGen turns a script, a photo or a short video into a presenter-led clip, then can translate it into 175+ languages. This review breaks down the credit math, the real plan limits and the jobs it handles better than a traditional studio.

The 30-second verdict on HeyGen

Quick verdict

HeyGen is worth shortlisting if you need avatar-led videos, multilingual lip-sync or SCORM-ready training content on Business, all without a studio. The credit system is the real decision variable: Avatar IV costs 16 credits per minute for a photo look and 31 for a video look, so the same plan buys very different run times. It is a weak fit if you need a permanent free tier or a simple per-minute price, because the Free plan stops at three watermarked, one-minute videos a month.

HeyGen at a glance

HeyGen is a web-based AI video platform. Its core workflow is straightforward: write or paste a script, choose an avatar or clone your own, pick a voice, and generate a video. The same account also handles video translation, voice cloning, AI-generated B-roll and, on Business, SCORM exports for learning management systems.

The product is built around avatars and short-form production rather than a traditional editing timeline. That makes it fast for talking-head updates, localized marketing clips and training modules, but it also means the credit cost changes with the model, the resolution and the type of avatar you use.

Who HeyGen fits — and who should skip it

Best for

  • Solo creators and social teams who want a presenter on screen without filming themselves.
  • Marketing and localization teams that need the same clip in several languages with lip-sync.
  • Learning and development teams that deliver training through a SCORM-compatible LMS.
  • Agencies producing avatar-led explainers or localized ads for clients.

Not best for

  • Buyers who want a permanent free plan with meaningful monthly output.
  • Teams that need a fixed per-minute price and cannot manage a credit budget.
  • Anyone who needs unlimited Avatar IV generation; HeyGen says no plan, including Enterprise, offers it.
  • Creators who only need occasional short clips and can use a simpler editor.

The verdict: what works and what grates

Pros

  • One subscription covers avatar video, translation, voice cloning and AI asset generation, so you are not stitching four tools together.
  • Avatar IV can animate a single photo, which makes a first talking-head video much cheaper than a studio shoot.
  • Translation covers 175+ languages and dialects, with audio-only, speed lip-sync and precision lip-sync modes.
  • Business adds SCORM export, LMS support and SAML/SSO, which is unusual in a self-serve video plan.
  • Annual billing lowers the effective monthly price to $24 for Creator and $41 for Pro.

Cons

  • Credit rates range from 4 to 58 credits per minute depending on the job, so comparison shopping takes arithmetic.
  • The Free plan is a trial quota: three videos a month, one minute each, with a watermark.
  • Creator users cannot buy a top-up; running out means moving to Pro.
  • Business seats cost $20 each per month but do not add credits; the 1,500-credit pool is shared.
  • 4K export and Avatar 6 Pro start at Pro; Creator is limited to 1080p and Avatar 6 Turbo.
  • Cancelling drops the account to Free and unused credits do not carry forward.

What one HeyGen credit actually buys

HeyGen's paid plans are sold in credits, not minutes. Each plan includes a monthly credit balance, and each feature deducts credits at its own rate. That is why two videos of the same length can cost very different amounts.

On the Creator plan, 600 credits buy roughly 37 minutes of Avatar IV photo video (16 credits per minute), 19 minutes of Avatar IV video video (31 credits per minute), 30 minutes of Avatar 6 Turbo (20 credits per minute), or 100 minutes of speed translation (6 credits per minute). Those figures assume every credit goes to one feature and ignore rollover, add-ons and any scene-level savings.

Three jobs HeyGen handles well

1. Avatar IV: a presenter from a photo or a short video

Avatar IV is the fastest route to a talking-head clip. A photo look costs 16 credits per minute; a video look costs 31. HeyGen says the engine analyzes vocal tone, rhythm and emotion to generate facial movement, and that Avatar IV always consumes credits — no plan, including Enterprise, includes unlimited Avatar IV.

  • Good for: quick product updates, personalized outreach, social explainers and internal announcements where a full shoot is overkill.
  • Script limit: HeyGen's Avatar IV guide lists a 5,000-character limit for photo-to-video scripts.
  • Extra motion: Custom Expressive Motion adds 40 credits per minute if you want more deliberate gestures.

2. Translation and lip-sync across 175+ languages

HeyGen's translator takes an existing video or a YouTube link and re-voices it while keeping the original speaker's voice and syncing lip movements. Paid plans cover 175+ languages and dialects; the Free plan lists 30+.

  • Three modes: audio only (4 credits per minute, no lip-sync), speed (6 credits per minute, best for front-facing footage) and precision (10 credits per minute, for complex shots, speaker changes and occlusions).
  • Batch size: HeyGen's translator page says you can choose up to 10 languages to translate simultaneously.
  • Official examples: 600 credits buy 100 minutes of speed translation, 1,000 credits buy 166 minutes and 1,500 credits buy 250 minutes.
  • Duration caps still apply: 30 minutes per video on Creator and Pro, 60 on Business, and no maximum on Enterprise.
  • Good for: localizing ads, turning a webinar into several language cuts, and giving an existing course a second market without a reshoot.

3. SCORM and LMS training on Business

Business adds SCORM export and LMS integrations. HeyGen's help center says you can export SCORM 1.2 or SCORM 2004 (4th Edition) packages, set a completion threshold, and optionally host the video on HeyGen so edits auto-update the SCORM content.

  • Good for: onboarding, compliance training, product education and any course that needs completion tracking inside an LMS.
  • Beyond video: Business also includes a screen recorder and interactive video features such as branching and quizzes.
  • The catch: SCORM is not on Creator or Pro, so budget for Business if LMS delivery is a hard requirement.

Where HeyGen gets awkward

  • Credit costs are model- and resolution-dependent. Avatar 6 Pro at 2K costs 58 credits per minute, while audio-only translation costs 4. A mixed project is harder to forecast than a per-minute editor.
  • The duration cap is separate from the credit balance. Creator and Pro cap each video at 30 minutes, Business at 60, and Enterprise removes the cap. Buying more credits does not raise the cap.
  • Business seats and credits are separate. Each extra seat costs $20 per month, but the 1,500-credit pool stays the same; only credit add-ons increase capacity.
  • Creator has no top-up path. If 600 credits run out, the only official route is a higher plan or Pro tier.
  • Cancellation forfeits unused credits. Access continues to the end of the billing cycle, then the account moves to Free; unused credits do not carry forward.
  • API usage is billed separately. Subscription credits do not cover API-generated video; HeyGen sells API credits on a pay-as-you-go basis.
  • Free is a test drive, not a working tier. Three videos a month, one minute each, with a watermark and no credit balance.
  • Account-level variation exists. HeyGen's credit article warns that some accounts may see different credit costs or limits for certain features.

When HeyGen is the right buy

  • You publish at least a few avatar videos a month and want cost to scale with output rather than seats. Creator's 600 credits cover about 37 minutes of Avatar IV photo video or 30 minutes of Avatar 6 Turbo.
  • You need the same clip in several languages and want lip-sync, not just subtitles. Speed mode is the cost-efficient default; precision is for complex footage.
  • You deliver training through an LMS and need SCORM packages. That requirement points to Business, not Creator or Pro.
  • You want a custom avatar of a real person and 4K output. Pro is the first plan that includes both.
  • You need SSO, shared billing and a team workspace. Business adds SAML/SSO and centralized billing.
  • You are building an automated video pipeline. Budget for API credits separately from the subscription.
